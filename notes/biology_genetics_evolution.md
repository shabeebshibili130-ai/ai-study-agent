# Biology: Genetics, Molecular Biology & Evolutionary Mechanisms

## 1. Molecular Genetics & The Central Dogma
- **DNA Structure**: Double helix (Watson, Crick, Franklin). Antiparallel strands ($5' \to 3'$ and $3' \to 5'$). Complementary base pairing: Adenine=Thymine (2 H-bonds), Guanine≡Cytosine (3 H-bonds).
- **DNA Replication**: Semiconservative (Meselson-Stahl). Helicase unwinds, DNA Polymerase III synthesizes leading strand continuously and lagging strand via Okazaki fragments, Ligase seals nicks.
- **Transcription**: RNA Polymerase binds promoter (TATA box), synthesizes pre-mRNA ($5' \to 3'$). Post-transcriptional processing: $5'$ methylguanosine cap, $3'$ poly-A tail, spliceosome intron removal.
- **Translation**: Ribosome reads mRNA codons via tRNA anticodons. Start codon: AUG (Methionine). Stop codons: UAA, UAG, UGA.

## 2. Mendelian & Modern Genetics
- **Mendel's Laws**:
  1. Law of Segregation: Two alleles for each gene separate during gamete formation (Meiosis I).
  2. Law of Independent Assortment: Genes on non-homologous chromosomes assort independently.
- **Non-Mendelian Patterns**: Incomplete dominance, codominance (ABO blood groups), sex-linked inheritance (hemophilia, color blindness on X chromosome), polygenic traits, epistasis.
- **Genetic Recombination & Linkage**: Recombination frequency $\theta = \frac{\text{recombinants}}{\text{total progeny}} \times 100\%$. $1\%$ recombination $= 1\text{ map unit (cM)}$.

## 3. Evolutionary Biology & Population Genetics
- **Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium**: Genotype frequencies remain constant across generations assuming:
  - Large population, random mating, no mutation, no gene flow, no natural selection.
  - Equations: $p + q = 1$ and $p^2 + 2pq + q^2 = 1$.
- **Mechanisms of Evolution**:
  - Natural Selection (directional, stabilizing, disruptive).
  - Genetic Drift (Bottleneck effect, Founder effect in small populations).
  - Gene Flow (migration) and Mutation (source of novel genetic variation).
- **Speciation**: Allopatric (geographic isolation) vs. Sympatric (polyploidy, behavioral, ecological niche differentiation).
